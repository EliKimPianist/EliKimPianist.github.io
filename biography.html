ELI SUNGHYUN KIM

Biography

Eli Sunghyun Kim is a South Korean pianist whose international activities span the United Kingdom, United States, Austria, South Korea, Japan, Taiwan, and Vietnam. She has appeared at distinguished venues and institutions including the Wiener Musikverein in Vienna, Carnegie Hall, Walt Disney Concert Hall, and Harvard University in the US, as well as in concert series and festivals throughout Asia.

In summer 2026, Eli was invited to Vietnam as a faculty member and guest artist, presenting performances and masterclasses at Harmony Bechstein Academy, Duc Tri, and S Talent Academy. Her international activities also extend to competition adjudication, with invitations to serve as a jury member for competitions in Hong Kong and Malaysia. Her performance projects and artistic collaborations include the Mozart Piano Sonata Complete Series and the French Music Project , presented across the United States, Taiwan, and South Korea.

Eli was awarded the Grand Prize in the Youngsan Cultural Foundation’s Professional Artists Development Project, receiving sponsorship from the Foundation. She was also a winner of the OpusOne International Music Festival Concerto Competition, which led to an invited concerto performance in California. Her honors additionally include the Korean Honor Scholarship from the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in the USA and a scholarship from the Seoul National University Alumni Association of the Chicago Area.

She is currently a Doctoral Fellow and Doctor of Musical Arts (DMA) candidate in Piano Performance and Literature at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, where she holds the Langford Fellowship . She received her Master of Music from Indiana University with the Artistic Excellence Award and her Bachelor of Music from Seoul National University with a university scholarship.

Working between London, Seoul, and Illinois , Eli continues an active international career as a pianist and researcher, with ongoing performances, artistic projects, and scholarly work across Europe, North America, and Asia.
